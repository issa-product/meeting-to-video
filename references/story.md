# Story: hook, structure, closer, cut (use this to write PLAN.md)

A meeting is a conversation; the video is a story. Read the whole packed transcript once before deciding anything.

## 1. Find the hook (first 3-15 seconds)
The hook is a REAL line from the recording that makes a coworker want to keep watching. Collect 3-5 candidates
while reading, then pick one with the user. Good hooks, best first:
- **Self-aware / funny**: the speaker comments on the meeting itself ("nobody wants to watch an hour of me
  talking about features"). Instantly human, and it sets a playful tone.
- **A surprising number**: "at least 10X", "40% of users", "4 days down to half a day".
- **A tension or problem**: "we know why, but we don't know why", "this is the tricky part".
- **A strong opinion**: "if everything's high, nothing's high".
- **A reaction**: the other person's genuine "this is so huge".
Avoid: generic intros ("so today I'll show you"), hype that isn't in the recording, anything that needs context.
A hook can be reused later as the payoff when the same line comes up in its real place (anchor with `after=`).
Treat it with a kinetic graphic (strike-through, pill, punchline landing on the word).

## 2. Structure
Pick the shape that matches the material, then name 4-8 chapters:
- **Walkthrough / roadmap**: hook → title → the big picture (all items, briefly) → each important item (why,
  what, demo) → what's next / open questions → closer.
- **Demo**: hook → the problem today (old way) → the new way (demo) → before vs after → what's next → closer.
- **Decision / update**: hook → context → options → decision + why → impact → next steps → closer.
- **Q&A heavy**: group questions by theme; each chapter = one question + its best answer.
Chapter titles: 2-4 words, plain and specific ("Offline mode", "What comes first"), a kicker line under each
(5-7 words, says why it matters). Never "Part 1".

## 3. Keep / trim / cut (the user's rule: never lose important information)
- **Keep**: every fact, number, decision, reason, deadline, owner, and the demo of anything new.
- **Trim**: the second and third time something is explained; long setup before a point; hedging ("I think,
  maybe, kind of") around a clear statement.
- **Cut**: audio checks, greetings, scheduling, small talk unrelated to the topic, demo failures and their
  apologies (keep what the demo was supposed to show; explain it with a graphic instead), "I'll re-record this",
  dead air.
- Keep a few human moments (a laugh, a good reaction): they make it fun. Cut them if they cost information time.
- Questions from the other person stay when the answer adds information; they become bubbles or "X asks" cards.

## 4. Length budget
| Target | Shape |
|---|---|
| under 3 min | hook + 3 chapters, one key point each, demo highlights only |
| about 5 min | hook + 4-6 chapters, main points + one demo stretch |
| about 7 min | hook + 6-8 chapters, most points + demos + a Q&A beat |
| keep everything important | whatever it takes; tell the user the expected length and what makes it long |
Estimate from the cut list (cards add ~2s each). If over target, show the user what you'd drop and let them choose.

## 5. The closer
End on energy, not on "okay, bye". Best: a fun exchange (a bet, a joke, a callback to the hook) → end card
(Questions? Ping <presenter> on Slack). Or: the single most important takeaway as a kinetic line. Cut the
goodbyes and scheduling after it.

## 6. Use the user's focus ideas
The 2-3 things the user wants viewers to remember get: their own chapter (or the hook), the strongest graphic,
and a mention in the end card or Slack post. Ask at each preview: "do these land?"

## 7. What goes in PLAN.md (the user approves it)
Section table (source time, what it is, keep / trim / cut, why), 3 hook candidates with your pick, the closer,
chapter names + kickers, the graphic idea for every section (no idle screen), estimated length vs target, what
you'd drop to hit the target, sensitive content, open questions (spellings, emphasis).
